## Cyber front

- **Norway's energy sector hit through a Microsoft Azure flaw (rising):** Norway's National Security Authority (NSM) says an unidentified actor exploited a flaw in older "Classic" tiers of Azure API Management to steal access tokens and secrets. Equinor and Skagerak Energi were affected, and production and power output continued normally. Microsoft says it found the flaw Nov 18 and mitigated it Nov 19, and tells customers to rotate their credentials (The Record, BleepingComputer). **Verified** by NSM, Microsoft and the victims. Attribution is unknown.
- **FBI NCIC database breach reported (rising):** Sources told CNN and AP that the National Crime Information Center was breached Thursday. Trump says he was briefed and called it "not good." The FBI hasn't commented (The Record). **Unverified** officially. If confirmed, it would be the second FBI breach this year.
- **CISA adds two to the KEV catalog (steady):** MONAI Label path traversal (the medical-imaging AI flaw reportedly found by AI agents), fixed in 0.8.6, and an N-able MailAssure/SpamExperts SSRF tied to the Mail Assure attack. Federal agencies must patch by Nov 26 (CISA). **Verified.**
- **Austrian Foreign Ministry site breached (steady):** Hackers reached the names and official contact details of staff at headquarters and embassies through a website run by an outside provider on Nov 12-13. The ministry says it has no attribution and that no public data was taken (The Record, ORF). **Verified** by the victim.
- **"Holiday cyber onslaught" hype (falling):** The FAA says the Nov 13 nationwide ground stop came from router upgrades flooding its network, with no outside actor. DNI Jay Clayton says the FAA, Microsoft and JPMorgan outages had different causes and weren't a coordinated attack, but expects more "bumps in the road" during upgrades (FAA, CNBC). Separately, NVIDIA is investigating a Gentlemen ransomware leak-site listing but says it has found no sign its own systems were hit (BleepingComputer). **Unverified** leak-site claim.

## Prep tip

If you already have an RTL-SDR, it can follow digital trunked public-safety radio with the free SDRTrunk software. A P25 Phase 1 control channel runs at 9,600 bps, and SDRTrunk follows it from talkgroup to talkgroup. One RTL-SDR covers only about 2.4 MHz at once, so every frequency on the site you want has to fit inside that window. Otherwise you need a second dongle or a wider receiver like an Airspy R2 (10 MHz, about $170-200). Encrypted talkgroups stay encrypted, so leave them alone, and check your state's rules on in-vehicle scanners. Action: look up your county's system on RadioReference.com and check whether its control and voice channels fit within 2.4 MHz.
