## Prep tip: run your roof's water math

- **Harvest numbers:** 1 inch of rain on 1,000 sq ft of roof footprint = **623 gallons**. Plan on about 75–80% capture (about 470–500 gal) after splash, gutter overflow and first flush.
- **First flush:** dump the first **~1 gallon per 100 sq ft** (10 gal for a 1,000 sq ft section). That's where the bird droppings and grit go.
- **Treat it like raw water:** Use safe tap water or bottled water for drinking and cooking. Keep collected roof runoff for uses like flushing toilets. If you’re considering drinking it, ask your local health department about testing and treatment for the germs and chemicals that may be present. Asphalt shingles are fine for washing and flushing but are a known contaminant source, while metal roofs are cleaner.
- **Action:** measure the footprint (length × width, not the slope) draining into one downspout, then do the math: area × 0.623 × inches of rain. That number tells you how many days of non-drinking water one storm buys you.
- **Upgrade (suggestions):** a used food-grade 275-gal IBC tote (often ~$100–200), a downspout first-flush diverter (~$30–50), and a cover to block light and mosquitoes. Check local rules first, since some areas regulate collection.
