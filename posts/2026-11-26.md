## Cyber front

- **SilentAir taps mobile networks (rising):** Enea says a state-level actor is using compromised nodes inside Asian mobile carriers to send silent SMS and SS7/Diameter signaling. It is aimed mainly at Taiwanese subscribers and can intercept SMS 2FA codes (Enea report and Nov 26 FAQ, The Hacker News). The activity is documented by Enea. The China link is low-confidence and **unverified**.
- **Salt Typhoon goes agentic (rising):** Microsoft says China's Salt Typhoon, plus North Korean operators, now run ARTEX, an open-source AI agent framework that automates recon, credential attacks and lateral movement (Microsoft Security blog, The Record). **Verified** by Microsoft's own telemetry. The attribution is Microsoft's.
- **Browser hijacks for email (new):** BleepingComputer reports SYS010 malware that abuses browser-management policies to force-install extensions that read Gmail and Outlook, plus a Remus stealer posing as a Google Sheets extension. This is a single-outlet report on researcher findings, so it's **unverified** beyond that.
- **Medical device makers on leak sites (new):** The Gentlemen gang lists artificial-heart maker SynCardia (claiming 1.5 TB) and a Siemens Healthineers cancer-care unit. Neither company has confirmed anything (BreachNews, Securlabs). **Unverified**, leak-site only.
- **Ransomware "recovery" firm charged (steady):** DOJ indicted the CTO of a Florida firm, RansomwareHelp. Prosecutors say it told victims it could decrypt files without paying, then secretly paid the attackers and charged victims a markup (DOJ, KrebsOnSecurity). **Verified** by the indictment, but these are allegations, not a conviction.
- **Hygiene:** On a personal computer, open chrome://management. If it says your browser is managed and you never set that up, remove any unknown policies and extensions, and change your email password from a clean device.

## Prep tip

SMS codes can be intercepted inside the phone network (see SilentAir), so move your email and bank logins to hardware security keys. Buy two FIDO2 keys (about $25-55 each), register both on each account, and keep the spare somewhere other than home. Then remove SMS as a fallback where the site allows it, because attackers simply pick the weakest login method left. Action: set up email first, since every other password reset goes through it.
