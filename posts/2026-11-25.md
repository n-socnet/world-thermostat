# World Thermostat, 2026-11-25

## World temp: Hot

- **Bab el-Mandeb / Djibouti / Camp Lemonnier (rising):** The Saudi-led coalition struck Hodeidah port and targets in Saada and Amran. The Houthis had threatened Saudi Red Sea ports, including Jeddah, if that happened, and told port workers and shipping companies to evacuate and pull out their vessels (Al Jazeera, AFP, Reuters). The Houthis say they hit Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery. Saudi Arabia says it downed four drones there and reported no casualties. Footage shows smoke, so whether the refinery took damage is **unverified** (The National, NBC). No attacks or alerts are reported at Lemonnier, and Djibouti has made no new statement.
- **Iran / Hormuz (rising):** The IRGC Navy says two tankers on "unauthorized routes" hit mines and caught fire, and Fars reports a third. No ships are named and no casualties reported. The claims are **unverified**, and the Joint Maritime Information Center calls the mine claims unconfirmed (Reuters/Arab News, Iran International). TradeWinds separately reports a Hong Kong-flagged tanker came under fire in Hormuz. Iran's army chief ordered tighter shipping restrictions. Kpler's preliminary November estimate of Hormuz flows is about 12 million barrels a day, and more ships are now crossing with their transponders off.
- **Ukraine / Russia (steady, high):** Russia launched 112 drones and Ukraine downed or suppressed 88. Attacks over the past day killed 9 across Ukraine, a DTEK thermal power plant stopped generating, and a Naftogaz gas site in Poltava shut down (Kyiv Independent, Euronews). The Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant lost external power and switched to diesel, its 17th loss this year (Al Jazeera). Ukraine hit three Russian refineries and says it damaged three MiG-31s and two Su-34s at Savasleyka airbase. That damage claim is **unverified**.
- **Taiwan (falling):** Taiwan's defense ministry tracked 1 PLA aircraft and 9 ships, down from 3 aircraft and 9 ships.
- **Fuel and freight:** Brent settled at $94.61, up 3.51% on the Houthi refinery claim. That's the biggest one-day gain since Nov 5 (Dow Jones/Morningstar, CNBC). The US gas average is $3.59, after 31 straight days of declines (AAA). Crude is rising and pump prices are falling, so expect the gap to close.
- **Outbreak watch:** DRC Bundibugyo Ebola is at 11,886 cases and 5,832 deaths as of Nov 22, per DRC's health ministry. WHO's last count was 11,613 cases (to Nov 17), and its latest outbreak notice now lists Kenya and Nigeria too (WHO). Still a WHO global emergency. Rising slowly.
- **Florida hazards (steady):** The St. Johns River at Sanford is around 8.4 ft, in major flood. Crest forecasts run from 8.5 to 8.7 ft, the high end on Thursday evening, which would be near the record (NWS via local outlets; the forecasts differ).
- **Market odds:** Polymarket priced "US x Iran effective ceasefire by Nov 30," meaning a 14-day pause in US strikes, at about 73-74%. That's a third-party snapshot, not checked live.

## Cyber front

- **Supermarket refrigeration controllers exploited (new):** CISA added 10 entries to its exploited-vulnerability catalog, among them three flaws in Danfoss AK-SM 800A refrigeration system managers, five in Mitel MiCollab, one in the Juniper Session Smart router and one in Xen Orchestra (CISA). **Verified.** That's an attack path into the systems that keep store food cold.
- **Guadeloupe hospital ransomware (rising):** The island's largest hospital is back to pen and paper. Surgeries are postponed and radiation therapy and some chemotherapy are suspended, while the ER and dialysis keep running. France's cyber agency ANSSI is probing a possible entry through a compromised IT supplier (The Record). The attack is **verified**. Who did it is unknown.
- **Todoist breach (new):** Todoist confirms an attacker used stolen credentials to reach its source code and cloud systems on Nov 22-23. About 6% of users had their email, password hash and account date taken, and some API and Slack tokens were pulled (TechCrunch, Todoist). **Verified.**
- **Keio rail breach grows (steady):** Japan's Keio says a September ransomware attack exposed data on more than 11 million people, including 1.6 million hotel guests' passport photos. The attack is linked to Qilin (Japan Times). The scope is **verified** by the victim.
- **GPS jamming over the North Sea (steady):** About 140 flights saw roughly 20 minutes of GPS interference on Nov 9. Hexbird placed the source near a Russian warship escorting a convoy, but a UK defence source rejected one reported attribution (Telegraph, LBC, Anadolu). No flights were disrupted. The source is **unverified**.
- **Hygiene:** Todoist users should rotate their password and API tokens, reconnect integrations, and move any keys or recovery phrases out of their tasks and comments.

## Prep tip

Make your own oral rehydration salts to WHO spec. Per liter of water: 2.6 g sodium chloride, 13.5 g glucose, 1.5 g potassium chloride and 2.9 g trisodium citrate. For moderate dehydration, WHO's Plan B gives about 75 mL per kg of body weight over 4 hours. Sports drinks have the wrong sodium-to-sugar ratio for diarrheal illness. Action: use a 0.01 g scale to make ten single-liter dry packets and label each with the recipe and the 75 mL/kg rule.
